## What is The "So What" Test?

The "So What" Test is a 3-step communication framework designed for technical SEO report writing. It bridges the gap between raw web diagnostics and executive decision-making by forcing every finding through three distinct stages:

![3 step frameworktitled "So What" test. Step 1 check. Step 2 Consequence. Step 3 action. ](/blog/article5-img.png)


1.  **The Check (Data):** What did the crawler, Google Search Console (GSC), or Google Analytics (GA4) find? *(This is the raw technical observation).*
    
2.  **The Consequence (Impact):** Why should a non-technical executive care? What is this technical flaw costing them in traffic, user friction, or revenue?
    
3.  **The Action (ROI):** What exact step will we take to resolve it, and what business metric will improve once it's done?
    

When you present technical SEO audit findings through this framework, you stop acting like a vendor delivering bug lists and start positioning yourself as a trusted advisor managing a revenue channel.

## 3 Real Examples: From Raw Crawl Data to Client Decisions

Let’s examine how to apply the **Check ➔ Consequence ➔ Action** framework to three common SEO audit findings, ranging from simple technical bugs to complex site architecture issues.

* * *

### Example 1: The Broken Link (Technical Flaw)

#### The Raw Data (The Check)

Your crawler flags a `404 Not Found` response code on a high-traffic subfolder page: `/services/commercial-roofing-repair`.

*   **The Bad "Standard" Report:**
    

> *"We ran an outaudits crawl and found a 404 error on* `/services/commercial-roofing-repair`*. We need to implement a 301 redirect immediately to clean up our crawl budget."*

**Client reaction:** *"What's a 301 redirect? Why are we paying you to fix budget errors on a crawler?"*

#### Applying The "So What" Test

![An infographic showing the 3 distinct stages every finding in a technical report the "So What" test subjects it to.](/blog/article5-img3.png)


*   **The Rewritten "So What" Finding:**
    

> **Fixing a Broken Lead Route on Commercial Roofing ($2,400/mo Pipeline Recovery)**
> 
> *   **What We Found:** Our health check identified that the main page for Commercial Roofing Repair is returning a dead "404 Page Not Found" error.
>     
> *   **The Business Impact:** According to Search Console, this broken page receives approximately 160 clicks a month from high-intent local searches. Right now, 100% of those prospective clients are landing on a blank page and bouncing to a competitor. At your average 5% form conversion rate and $300 service value, this single broken link is quietly costing you **~$2,400 every month**.
>     
> *   **The Fix:** We are implementing a 301 permanent redirect to point that incoming search traffic to your live commercial services page today.
>     

* * *

### Example 2: Keyword Cannibalization (On-Page / Content)

#### The Raw Data (The Check)

Google Search Console shows two distinct URLs (`/blog/seo-tips` and `/services/seo-agency`) ranking for the primary search query *"SEO Agency Services,"* fluctuating between positions #8 and #14.

*   **The Bad "Standard" Report:**
    

> *"Keyword cannibalization detected between* `/blog/seo-tips` *and* `/services/seo-agency`*. Both URLs are competing for the intent 'SEO Agency Services.' We need to consolidate canonical tags."*

**Client reaction:** *"Cannibalization sounds scary, but if both pages are on page 2 anyway, does it really matter?"*

#### Applying The "So What" Test

*   **The Rewritten "So What" Finding:**
    

> **Consolidating Competing Pages to Push Into the Top 3 Search Results**
> 
> *   **What We Found:** You currently have two separate pages on your site competing for the exact same key term (*"SEO Agency Services"*).
>     
> *   **The Business Impact:** Because Google doesn't know which page is more authoritative, it is splitting your search visibility in half. Neither page can break onto Page 1, trapping your site at position #9. Position #9 gets an average **1.5% click-through rate**, whereas pushing a single unified page into position #3 gets an average **11% click-through rate**—effectively **7x-ing your organic site visitors** for that term without spending more money on ads.
>     
> *   **The Fix:** We will merge the top content from the blog post directly into your main services page, redirect the old blog post, and tell Google clearly which page to rank.
>     

* * *

### Example 3: Core Web Vitals & Mobile Performance (UX / Technical)

#### The Raw Data (The Check)

Outaudits shows a Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) of 4.2 seconds and an Interaction to Next Paint (INP) of 380ms on the main mobile checkout page.

*   **The Bad "Standard" Report:**
    

> *"The mobile checkout URL failed Google's Core Web Vitals. LCP is currently 4.2s (Red) and INP is 380ms (Yellow). We need a developer sprint to optimize render-blocking JavaScript and defer unneeded CSS."*

**Client reaction:** *"Our developers are busy with product features. Is 4 seconds really hurting us that much?"*

#### Applying The "So What" Test

![Illustration of the check - consequence - action framework applied to Core Web Vitals.](/blog/article5-img4.png)


*   **The Rewritten "So What" Finding:**
    

> **Optimizing Mobile Page Load to Capture $5,000/mo in Abandoned Checkouts**
> 
> *   **What We Found:** Your mobile checkout page currently takes **4.2 seconds** to load completely on smartphones.
>     
> *   **The Business Impact:** Google and mobile UX benchmarks show that every additional second of load time drops conversions by up to 20%. Looking at our GA4 data, your desktop traffic converts at **3.1%**, but your mobile traffic lags at **1.2%**. Mobile users are getting frustrated waiting for images to load and abandoning their carts.
>     
> *   **The Fix:** We are compressing unoptimized hero images and deferring heavy tracking scripts. This will bring load speeds under 2.0 seconds, aiming to lift mobile conversion rates closer to desktop levels and capture an estimated **+$5,000/month in previously abandoned revenue**.
>

## How to Package the Deliverable: The "1-Page Executive Summary"

If you send a client a 30-page audit—even if every finding is written using the "So What" framework—they will still get overwhelmed.

To ensure maximum client comprehension and rapid sign-off, condense your findings into a **1-Page Executive Deliverable** structured around an **Impact vs. Effort Matrix**:

### The 1-Page Template Architecture

1.  **Executive Health Score (0–100):** A single visual gauge showing site progression over time (e.g., **Green 84/100 — Up +12 pts from baseline**).
    
2.  **Top 3 High-Impact Priorities (This Month's Focus):**
    

*   🔴 **Critical Blocker (High Impact / Low Effort):** Fix 404 lead route on `/services` *(Pipeline Recovery: $2,400/mo)*.
    
*   🟡 **Growth Accelerator (High Impact / High Effort):** Consolidate cannibalizing service pages *(Traffic Lift: +300%)*.
    
*   🔵 **UX Optimization (Medium Impact / Low Effort):** Compress mobile checkout images *(Conversion Lift: +0.8%)*.
    

3.  **What We Fixed Last Month (Proof of ROI):** A brief bulleted list of completed items tied to business results.
    

When a client receives a 1-page document organized by financial impact and prioritization, they don't ask "What am I paying for?" They ask: **"How fast can your team execute these three fixes?"**
